# Story Illustration Quality Gates

Use these gates for every new or existing cover and comprehension image. A correctly named,
technically valid file may still fail.

## Blocking white-field vignette and calm-palette gate

Apply this gate first to every cover and comprehension image. Record each item as `PASS` or
`FAIL`; any failure blocks acceptance.

1. **White field:** Pure white remains connected to all four edges around the illustration.
2. **No scenic backdrop:** No room, wall, window, corner, broad floor plane, furniture grouping,
   horizon, landscape, background rectangle, colored panel, circle, blob, or decorative frame.
3. **Tight focal cluster:** The person, animal, object, relationship, or action is the immediate
   read and carries roughly 70-85% of the visual attention.
4. **Minimal cues:** Zero to two support cues are present, and each is necessary to understand or
   physically support the action.
5. **Anchor match:** A contact sheet beside two approved Level 1-2 covers and two level cards shows
   the same simple white-field composition at full size and app-card size.
6. **Calm palette match:** At full size and app-card size, the image matches the anchors' restrained
   saturation, gentle contrast, and calm color balance. It contains no large areas or repeated
   accents of fire-engine red, lemon yellow, vivid orange, candy/neon primaries, or multiple
   competing high-saturation warm colors. A prose-required red/yellow/orange object remains
   recognizable through a muted storybook variant and is balanced with neutrals.

The rare exception requires explicit human approval before generation and must be recorded in the
story prompt history. Story setting alone is not an exception. If the image reads as “a room or
landscape containing characters,” fail it; it should read as “the character, object, relationship,
or action.”

## Cover gates

A cover passes only when all are true:

1. **Story truth:** It depicts a moment, action, relationship, or outcome established by the
   approved prose. It does not add an essential fact the child could learn only from art.
2. **Specific hook:** It expresses the approved child hook or signature visual beat, not a
   generic character beside a relevant prop.
3. **Single focal action:** A child can point to what is happening immediately at card size.
4. **Readable feeling:** Pose, face, gaze, and spacing communicate the intended surprise,
   curiosity, pride, worry, relief, warmth, or comedy without relying on symbols or text.
5. **Right energy:** Delight-first stories feel playful or surprising; warm stories feel
   connected; serious stories remain gentle and clear. Do not force zany energy into every
   story.
6. **Continuity:** Character design, relationships, possessions, setting, and physical facts
   agree with the series and character bibles.
7. **House-style match:** Side-by-side comparison with the required Level 1-2 covers and level
   cards confirms restrained saturation, gentle contrast, softened dark outlines, low-contrast
   shading, rounded proportions, and the white-field vignette composition. A factually correct
   image still fails if it looks noticeably hotter, harsher, brighter, more saturated, glossier,
   more realistic, or scenic. Colorfulness is not a substitute for a playful pose or action.
8. **Young proportions:** Children read immediately as ages 5-8, with rounded faces and compact,
   age-appropriate bodies. Reject tall, slender, teen-like, fashion-illustration, or adult-like
   proportions.
9. **Vignette cohesion:** Characters and props share believable scale, light, contact, overlap,
   and spatial relationships. Use a small shadow or essential support fragment when needed. Never
   add an environment to manufacture cohesion.
10. **Craft:** Composition, line work, color, anatomy, hands, eyes, props, grounding, margins,
   and artifacts withstand full-size inspection.

## Comprehension-set gates

Judge the three answers as one set:

- Each image depicts the literal choice as the question asks it: person, action, object,
  place, or outcome.
- Choices use comparable scale, framing, detail, finish, emotional energy, and background.
- Every choice independently passes the blocking white-field vignette gate.
- Choices match the approved house style as a set: restrained saturation, gentle contrast,
  softened outline weight, young proportions, equivalent scale, and equivalent minimal-cue treatment.
- Every choice passes `CALM_PALETTE_MATCH`; no choice uses a hotter or brighter palette to attract
  attention, and required named colors use the same calm storybook treatment across the set.
- No check marks, glows, pointing gestures, exaggerated happiness, richer detail, or other
  correctness cues favor the right answer.
- The images differ only as much as the answer meanings require.
- Every prop is supported and every action is physically legible.

## Reuse and revision verdict

Open each existing candidate before deciding:

- `ACCEPT`: all relevant gates pass; preserve it.
- `REVISE`: the story moment is right and a bounded edit can fix expression, framing,
  grounding, continuity, or an artifact.
- `REPLACE`: the concept is generic, factually wrong, misleading, visually weak, scenic, or depends
  on a different central moment. Also replace a candidate whose background structure, detail load,
  palette, contrast, outline weight, or proportions materially depart from the approved house style,
  including one that is noticeably hotter, brighter, or more saturated than the Level 1-2 anchors;
  do not rely on color correction to repair a structural style mismatch.

Record evidence, not adjectives: name the focal action, visible emotion, story-specific
detail, and any defect. Do not accept “cute,” “calm,” or “high quality” as a complete review.
